# Adapting to another niche

Create a short project profile covering audience, promise, tone, evidence standards, narrative structure, visual grammar, target duration, and exclusions. Keep shared timing, storyboard, animation, and mastering mechanics unchanged unless the new format actually requires a change.

| Niche | Story engine | Research and visual change |
|---|---|---|
| Company history | An origin becomes a scalable system, then meets a limit | Archives, filings, regulators; timelines and money or product flows |
| Technology history | A technical constraint leads to successive inventions | Papers, patents, museums; labeled mechanism diagrams and dated interfaces |
| Historical biography | A person's choices within documented circumstances | Archives and primary correspondence; distinguish reconstruction from evidence |
| Science explanation | A question leads through a mechanism to a supported answer | Papers and scientific institutions; show assumptions and uncertainty |
| Infrastructure | A physical bottleneck shapes a place or system | Planning records and engineering sources; maps, sections, and flows |
| Sports history | A rule, rivalry, or institution changes competition | Official results and archives; score and era labels, original action diagrams |

Choose structure because it fits the subject. Science does not need a corporate scandal chapter. A biography does not need a market-cap ending. The original speculative seven-beat formula is relevant only when the user requests a short hypothetical story; do not turn documented history into a fictional scenario.

When new visual conventions are requested, create a compact style sheet with palette, typography, recurring shapes, and examples. Retain pure frame functions, explicit timing, source traceability, and storyboard review. Add tools or dependencies only for a concrete requirement and disclose paid calls before use when the existing request does not already cover them.
